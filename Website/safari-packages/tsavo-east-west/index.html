Package Overview

About this safari

Tsavo is enormous, and its two halves behave like different parks. East is open red earth and the Galana river, known for dust-red elephants and the long Yatta plateau. West is denser bush, volcanic hills and Mzima Springs, where hippo can be watched through a glass tank. Four days covers both without rushing the drives between them.

Best for: Travellers driving between Nairobi and the coast, or wanting a crowd-free Big Five trip.

About Tsavo National Parks

Together, Tsavo East and Tsavo West form one of the largest protected wildlife sanctuaries on the planet, spanning nearly 22,000 square kilometers between Nairobi and the Kenyan coast. Divided by the railway and highway, the two parks offer starkly distinct yet complementary safari landscapes. Tsavo East is celebrated for its vast, rolling semi-arid plains, the 300-km-long Yatta Plateau (the world's longest lava flow), Lugard Falls, and the iconic 'Red Elephants of Tsavo'—magnificent herds stained ochre-red from the volcanic soil. Tsavo West features dramatic volcanic topography, including the jagged Shetani Lava Flow, Roaring Rocks, and Mzima Springs, where fifty million gallons of crystal-clear spring water gush daily into pools with an underwater glass viewing chamber for hippos and fish.

Day by Day

Your Itinerary

A working outline. Every day can be re-paced, extended or swapped for a lodge you prefer.

  1. Day 1

    Nairobi to Tsavo East

    Drive via Mtito Andei and the Buchuma gate, arriving for lunch. Afternoon game drive along the Galana river.

  2. Day 2

    Full day in Tsavo East

    Dawn drive for elephant and lion, Lugard Falls and the long waterholes through the middle of the day, sundowners at a viewpoint.

  3. Day 3

    Cross to Tsavo West

    Morning drive, then across to Tsavo West via Manyani. Afternoon at Mzima Springs, then a drive through the Shetani lava flows.

  4. Day 4

    Morning drive and return

    Early drive toward the Chyulu hills or Kilaguni waterhole, breakfast, then the road back to Nairobi.
